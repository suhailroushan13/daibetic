## The system architecture

CGM → algorithm → insulin pump → body → CGM. The sensor estimates interstitial glucose. The algorithm predicts near-term behavior within safety constraints. The pump delivers insulin, which takes time to absorb and act. The resulting glucose feeds back into the next decision. <Citation id="ada-technology" />

## Delays and disturbances

Meals, exercise, stress, illness and infusion-site changes disturb the system. Once insulin is delivered, the algorithm cannot recall it. A sensor has noise and lag. Many systems still require user input or meal announcements; “closed loop” does not necessarily mean fully autonomous daily life.

## What it solves—and what remains

Automated delivery can improve glucose management and reduce burden for appropriate users. It does not restore beta cells or stop autoimmunity. Connectivity, supply access, training, hypoglycemia prevention and a backup plan remain essential. <Citation id="crises" />

<Callout type="analogy" title="Control theory — interpretation">This resembles a constrained controller with delayed actuation and imperfect state estimation. Safe design optimizes glucose outcomes while limiting the harm of prediction errors.</Callout>
